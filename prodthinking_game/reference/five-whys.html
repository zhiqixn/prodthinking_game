Five Whys

Move from symptom to cause before choosing a solution.

How

  1. Write the symptom: what goes wrong, for whom.
  2. Ask “why?”. Write the answer as the next line.
  3. Ask “why?” about that answer. Repeat.
  4. Stop when you reach a cause you can remove. Five is a rule of thumb, not a quota.
Sources: IDG, Start With The Whys; Lean Enterprise Institute.

Pick the level to fix

IDG’s two tests:

  • Within your control
  • Closely connected to the outcome you want to change

Too shallow: the problem returns. Too deep: you can’t act on it.

Watch for

  • Stopping at the first fix. Replacing the fuse means the failure recurs.
  • Treating five as the rule. Keep going until you reach a removable cause.
  • Assuming one root cause. Problems rarely have just one, so chains branch.
  • Chains that blame people. Ask why again, until you reach a process you can change.
The first two come from the Lean Enterprise Institute and the third from IDG. The fourth is this course’s own teaching note.

Example (Ohno)

  1. SymptomThe machine stopped.
  2. Why 1An overload blew the fuse.
  3. Why 2The bearing wasn’t lubricated enough.
  4. Why 3The pump wasn’t pumping enough.
  5. Why 4The pump shaft was worn.
  6. Root causeNo strainer, so metal scraps got in.
Example as given by the Lean Enterprise Institute, citing Ohno (1988).
